# Account and plans

<div className="lead">
  Native VM is used signed in. Your account decides your plan, Free or Pro, and the app asks for it
  each time it starts.
</div>

## Creating an account

Accounts are made on toolxbd.com. Choose **Create an account** on Native VM's sign-in window, or go
to [toolxbd.com/register](https://www.toolxbd.com/register). Native VM never sees that form. Once the
account exists, come back and sign in with the same email and password.

## Signing in

Native VM opens on its sign-in window, and no server window opens until you've signed in and your
access has been checked. While it checks, the window says **Checking your access**.

Your session is kept where your credentials are kept, the OS keychain unless you chose otherwise:
your email and a session token, never your password. The next time you open Native VM, it checks the
session's access again and opens straight onto your servers.

| What the account service answers | What happens |
|---|---|
| Your access is fine | Your servers open. |
| Your session has ended | You're asked to sign in again, with your email filled in. |
| Your account has no access | You see the service's reason, and the sign-in form. |
| No answer, a timeout or an untrusted certificate | A dialog says why, with **Try again** or **Quit**. Your session is kept. |

<Callout type="note">
  Native VM talks to the internet in two places only: the account service, when you sign in and when
  the app starts, and the update check, when you ask for it. Nothing about your servers is sent to
  either.
</Callout>

## Signing out

Open the account menu at the right of any window's top bar and choose **Sign out**. After you
confirm, Native VM removes the session, closes every server window, disconnecting each server, and
shows the sign-in window.

## Free and Pro

| | Free | Pro |
|---|---|---|
| Servers | 2 | No limit |
| Apps created on each server | 2 | No limit |
| Cron jobs on each server | 1 | No limit |
| Terminal | Yes | Yes |
| Wizard's advanced settings: commands, limits, environment | Yes | Yes |
| An app's environment variables | Yes | Yes |
| Advanced mode | No | Yes |
| Live Monitor and Uninstaller | No | Yes |
| Files outside the apps: Home, logs, nginx config | No | Yes |
| New apps from a private Git repository | No | Yes |

The top bar's account says **Free** or **Pro**. On Free, the Servers window shows how much of the
plan is in use, and features that need Pro offer **Upgrade to Pro**, which opens toolxbd.com.

- **Limits are on creating, not on keeping.** Apps already on a server stay manageable however many
  there are. Cron jobs made while you were on Pro stay listed and editable.
- **Servers belong to this computer**, shared by every account signed in on it. On Free, the two
  servers added first open; the rest are listed, locked, until one of the two is removed or the
  account is Pro.

### Checking your plan again

Bought Pro while the app was open? Choose **Check my plan again** from the account menu. The new
plan reaches every window at once.

## Easy mode and Advanced mode

Every server window has an **Advanced mode** switch at the foot of its sidebar. Easy mode keeps the
window about your apps; Advanced mode adds the rest of the server:

- **Live Monitor** and **Uninstaller** in the sidebar,
- **All services** on the Services page, not only your apps',
- **Home, logs and nginx config** in the file manager,
- **Private Git repositories** in the New app wizard,
- **More than one cron job** on a server.

Advanced mode needs Pro. On Pro, choosing anything that needs it turns it on for you. On Free, those
pages stay listed with a lock and offer Pro instead.

<Callout type="note">
  Plans are enforced by the app on your own computer, so they are a product boundary, not a security
  one. Your servers never depend on your plan: apps keep running whatever it is.
</Callout>
